Information Protection & Security

Understanding Threats, Risks, and Controls

Importance & Classification

In business, information is a critical asset that differentiates companies and provides leverage.

Information Lifecycle Management

Proper classification involves managing data through various stages:

  • Labeling: Headers/footers specifying handling.
  • Distribution: Controlling who sees the data.
  • Duplication: How copies are made and handled.
  • Release: How it is provided to outsiders.
  • Storage & Encryption: Where it is kept and if it is encrypted.
  • Disposal: Shredding or wiping data.
  • Method of transmission: email, fax, print and mail .

Classification Levels

1. Top Secret

Highest level of national security classification.

2. Secret

Mid-level classification for sensitive data.

3. Confidential

Basic level of classified information.

Unclassified

Information not meeting classification requirements.

Risk Analysis

Risk analysis for organizations is the process of identifying, assessing, and prioritizing risks to minimize the impact of potential threats. This involves evaluating the probability of occurrence, potential impact on the organization, and determining appropriate measures to manage or mitigate these risks. Effective risk analysis helps organizations protect their assets, maintain compliance with regulations, and ensure business continuity.

The 5 Steps of Risk Analysis

  1. Identification: Determining what could go wrong.
  2. Assessment: Analyzing likelihood and impact.
  3. Prioritization: Ranking risks by severity.
  4. Mitigation: Strategies to manage/reduce risk.
  5. Monitoring: Continuous review.

Key Frameworks

  • NIST RMF: Risk Management Framework.
  • ISO 31000: Risk Management Guidelines.
  • COSO ERM: Enterprise Risk Management.

Threats vs. Attacks

Threat: A circumstance with the potential to cause harm (intentional or accidental).

A threat in the context of information security refers to any circumstance or event that has the potential to cause harm to an information system or organization. Threats can exploit vulnerabilities to compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information and systems. They can originate from various sources and can be intentional or accidental

Aspects of Threats

  • Origin and Nature: Threats can originate from various sources and may be either intentional (malicious) or accidental.
  • Threat vectors: This is the specific path or means by which a threat gains access to a computer system or network to deliver a malicious outcome.
  • Sources and Targets: Identifying who is initiating the threat and what specific assets are being aimed at.
  • Malicious Mobile Code: Identifying who is initiating the threat and what specific assets are being aimed at.
  • Advanced Persistent Threats: Sophisticated, long-term attacks typically carried out by well-resourced groups.

Attack: A deliberate attempt to exploit vulnerabilities.

Common Attack Vectors

In information security, an attack refers to any deliberate attempt to gain unauthorized access to a system, network, application, or data to cause harm, steal information, disrupt operations, or exploit vulnerabilities. Attacks can be carried out by individuals, groups, or organizations with malicious intent (known as attackers or threat actors)

DoS / DDoS

Overloading a system to disrupt availability.

Malware

Viruses, worms, and Trojans infecting systems.

Phishing

Deceptive emails to steal sensitive info.

Ransomware

Encrypting data and demanding payment.

Zero-Day

Leveraging a vulnerability before it is patched.
